 Full Content

 Opening: From Justification to Confession

Bro. Darius began by highlighting our natural impulse to defend wrong choices rather than own them. Reflecting on David’s sin with Bathsheba and Uriah, he noted, “David didn’t point blame at others; he confronted his sin and repented.” True obedience starts with personal confession.

 1. Examples of Prompt Obedience

 Abraham’s Departure (Gen 12:1–5): Obeyed God’s call to leave his home without delay.
 Jericho March (Josh 6:1–5): Joshua and Israel followed God’s unusual strategy exactly.

> “Obedience doesn’t wait for understanding; it trusts God’s Word above all.”

 2. Disobedience as Sin

 Separation (Rom 6:23): Sin’s wages is death—disobedience is a breach of God’s design.
 Unbelief (John 3:16–18): Rejecting Christ’s gift is rebellion against God’s offer of salvation.

 3. Cultivating Obedience

1. Repentance & Restoration (1 John 1:9): Confess sin to be cleansed and restored.
2. Walk by the Spirit (Gal 5:16): Depend on the Holy Spirit to overcome fleshly desires.
3. Prayer for Alignment (Luke 22:42): Echo Jesus: “Not my will, but Yours be done.”
4. Fellowship & Accountability (Heb 10:24–25): Community strengthens and encourages obedience.

 4. Benefits of Obedience

 Divine Protection (Exod 12:1–14): God’s safeguarding of His people.
 Blessings & Provision (Deut 28:1–14): Obedience unleashes covenantal favor.
 Spiritual Intimacy (John 14:15–16): Keeping commands draws us closer to Jesus.
 Favor in Crisis (Dan 6:25–28): Daniel’s faithfulness led to miraculous deliverance.
 Eternal Fellowship (John 15:10): Obedience secures lasting life with God.



Key Quote: “When we cease defending our sin and instead confess and obey, God’s power transforms us.”

 Prayer Focus

 Confess areas where we’ve justified sin.
 Ask for Spirit-led strength to obey daily.
 Seek accountability in community.
 Surrender personal agendas to God’s will.
